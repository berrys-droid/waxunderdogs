The disclosure standard

Every release says who did what.

  1. Element by element. Lyrics, melody, harmony and structure, arrangement, vocal, instrumentation — each one attributed to a person or to a model. Not "made with AI." Which parts.
  2. Sources named. Public-domain texts, traditional material and the traditions they come from are credited, and nothing here claims to own a tradition it borrowed.
  3. A real voice is named as a real voice. Where a person sings, they are credited. Where a model sings, it says so. Where a model was trained on or rendered from someone's voice, that person consents in writing and the credit says that too.
  4. Corrections in public. We have already got credits wrong and fixed them where everyone could see. That will happen again, and it will be handled the same way.

This is not a disclaimer. It is the reason to trust anything else on this page.

For artists — the door is open

Wax Underdogs releases other people's music too. If you are making AI-assisted work and you are willing to say exactly how, there is a home for it here. What we offer is distribution, a release process that has already made the mistakes, and a label name that means something specific about honesty.

What we ask of a release:

01You wrote something real — the words, the music, or both — and you can say which.
02You can account for every element of the recording, and you will let it be published that way.
03Any borrowed or traditional material is named, and any real person's voice used in the making has consented in writing.
04You are willing to be corrected in public if something turns out to be wrong.

If that reads as a lot to ask, it is. It is also the entire product.
